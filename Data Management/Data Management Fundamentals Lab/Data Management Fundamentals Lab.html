Storage Systems

Learn about different data storage systems and their applications.

🗃️ Relational Databases (SQL)

Store data in tables with columns and rows, allowing efficient organization and retrieval.

Relational Databases
SQL

✅ Pros

  • Data organized efficiently
  • Tables can be linked
  • Standardized SQL language
  • ACID compliance

❌ Cons

  • Not ideal for massive unstructured data
  • Complex relationships increase complexity
  • Schema rigidity
Typical Use Cases:
• Financial systems
• Inventory management
• Customer relationship management (CRM)
• Transaction processing systems
📄 NoSQL Databases

Flexible, schema-less databases ideal for semi-structured or rapidly evolving data.

MongoDB
Document Database

✅ Pros

  • Schema-less flexibility
  • Rich querying capabilities
  • Horizontal scaling
  • JSON-like documents

❌ Cons

  • May not be ideal for complex queries on large datasets
  • Additional configuration for strong consistency
  • No built-in joins
Typical Use Cases:
• Content management systems
• Mobile applications
• Real-time analytics
• Catalogs and user profiles

Wide-Column Store

✅ Pros

  • Flexible partitioning

❌ Cons

  • Complex setup and management
  • Limited querying capabilities
  • Eventual consistency model
Typical Use Cases:
• Time-series data
• Internet of Things (IoT) applications
• High-write throughput systems
• Messaging systems

🛡️ Security Threats & Vulnerabilities

Understanding and defending against attempts by malicious actors to steal, corrupt, or disrupt data.

🦠 Malware

Viruses, ransomware, and other malicious software that infiltrate systems to steal data or cause havoc.

🎣 Phishing

Deceptive emails or messages that trick users into revealing sensitive information or clicking harmful links.

📡 Unsecured Networks

Public Wi-Fi and other unsecured networks that expose your data to anyone on the network.

🔑 Weak Passwords

Simple or reused passwords that make systems vulnerable to brute-force attacks.

🔐 Encryption Methods

Techniques for scrambling data to make it unreadable without proper authorization.

🔑
Symmetric Encryption

Uses a single key for both encryption and decryption.

Pros:
• Fast encryption/decryption
• Simple implementation

Cons:
• Secure key distribution needed
• Compromised key endangers all data
🔑🔓
Asymmetric Encryption

Uses public and private key pairs for secure communication.

Pros:
• Secure communication over open networks
• No pre-shared keys required

Cons:
• Slower processing
• Not ideal for large data volumes

1. Data Governance

2. Data Security

3. Access Controls

Restrict who can access and modify data based on roles.

📜 Data Privacy Regulations

Key regulations governing data collection, processing, and protection.

GDPR
European Union

General Data Protection Regulation - European Union law for keeping online information safe.

Key Rights:
• Right to know what data companies have
• Right to edit your data
• Right to be forgotten (data deletion)
• Right to data portability

Penalties: Up to 4% of company's yearly earnings
CCPA
California, USA

California Consumer Privacy Act - Gives Californians control over their personal information.

Key Rights:
• Right to know what's collected
• Right to opt out of data sale
• Right to access collected data
• Right to request data deletion

Penalties: $2,500 to $7,500 per violation
COPPA
United States

Children's Online Privacy Protection Act - Focuses on protecting children's data.

Requirements:
• Extra verification for children under 13
• Clear disclosure of data collection
• Parental permission required
• Clear privacy policies
• Secure data storage

Penalties: Up to $50,000 per violation
